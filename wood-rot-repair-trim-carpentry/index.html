The Difference

Most painters skip this. We don't.

Plenty of crews spray color over tired wood and move on. Rotted siding and trim keep failing under a fresh coat, and the paint peels within a season or two.

Wood rot starts where water gets in and stays: window and door trim, fascia boards behind the gutters, soffits under the eaves, and the bottom courses of siding. Left alone, soft wood spreads, invites pests, and lets moisture reach the framing behind it.

We handle the repair as its own trade, not an afterthought. We find the rot, trace the moisture source, cut out and replace the damaged wood, then seal and prime it so it's ready for paint. That's the part that protects your investment, and it's why a Brake repaint holds up. When the wood is sound again, we move into exterior house painting with a surface worth painting.

Repair Process

From rot to solid wood

A straightforward process that fixes the cause, not just the surface.

  1. 1

    Inspect & diagnose

    We probe for soft, spongy, or rotted wood and find where the moisture is getting in.

  2. 2

    Remove & replace

    Cut out the damaged wood and replace it with new, properly-sized material that matches the profile.

  3. 3

    Seal & prime

    Caulk the seams, prime the new wood, and close the gaps so water stays out for good.

  4. 4

    Paint to match

    Blend the repair into the existing finish so it disappears into the rest of the house.

Why It Matters

Fix the wood, protect the paint

Repairing the wood first is what makes the difference between a repaint that lasts and one that peels.

  • Keeps moisture out

    Sound, sealed wood stops water before it reaches the framing behind your siding.

  • Paint that lasts

    A solid surface holds the coating, so the finish stays put instead of peeling in a season.

  • Protects your investment

    Catching rot early costs far less than replacing framing or siding down the road.
